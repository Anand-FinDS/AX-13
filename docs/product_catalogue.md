# Product Catalogue

> Illustrative policy document for the AX-13 prototype. Not Paytm policy. Product terms are placeholders. Numbers marked (illus.) are prototype settings, listed for tuning in Autonomy policy §6.

This catalogue is for the Grow line: merchants who are healthy and ready for the next step. Every section answers three questions: who it suits, which signals show readiness, and what not to promise. AX-13 cites the section it pitched from (for example "Product catalogue §2").

The Save line comes first. A merchant with an open Save case gets nothing from this catalogue (Retention playbook §1.1).

---

## 1. Activation ladder

**1.1 The ladder.** Merchants move up one step at a time:

1. **QR**: accepting UPI at all.
2. **Soundbox**: payments confirmed out loud, fewer disputes at the counter.
3. **Steady volume history**: months of regular digital sales on record.
4. **Loan eligibility**: assessed by a partner lender, who decides.

Each step builds the next. The Soundbox makes UPI easy to accept. Regular UPI builds a record. A lender can only assess a record that exists.

**1.2 Readiness signals.**

| Next step | Ready when |
|---|---|
| Soundbox | At least 10 UPI payments a day for 4 weeks (illus.), no Soundbox yet |
| Loan path | At least 90 days of steady volume history (illus.), Soundbox in place, no open Save case |
| POS | High-ticket category (pharmacy, restaurant, electronics, salon) with customers asking to pay by card |

**1.3 Never skip a step.** No loan pitch to a merchant doing 2 UPI payments a day. A thin history is a reason to grow digital volume first, not to pitch credit.

---

## 2. Soundbox

**Who it suits.** Busy counters where the owner cannot check the phone for every payment: kiranas, chai stalls, food stalls, pharmacies.

**Readiness signals.** Steady UPI volume (§1.2), QR only, disputes or tickets about "payment not received", peak-hour crowds.

**What it costs.** Monthly rental of ₹99–199 (illus.), deducted from the first settlement of the month and shown in the deductions list.

**What not to promise.** It does not reduce fees, raise sales by itself or affect loan eligibility. Do not say "free" if a rental applies.

---

## 3. POS (card machine)

**Who it suits.** Higher-ticket merchants whose customers want to pay by card: pharmacies, restaurants, electronics, salons.

**Readiness signals.** Category fit, high average ticket, lost sales or requests for card payment mentioned in visits or calls.

**What not to promise.** Card payments carry merchant charges. State them. Do not present POS as a way around the UPI fee.

---

## 4. Merchant loan via partner lenders

**Who it suits.** Merchants with a steady digital sales history who need working capital, for example festive stock.

**Readiness signals.** Loan path reached on the ladder (§1.2), stated intent (often heard by VoiceCore, for example "loan for festive stock"), no open Save case.

**How it works.**
- AX-13 never makes a loan offer on its own. It prepares the case and an RM approves (Compliance rules §5).
- The partner lender decides eligibility, amount, tenure and rate. Paytm distributes; it does not decide.
- Every offer carries the full disclosures in Compliance rules §1: lender name, amount, tenure, APR, all charges, Key Fact Statement link.
- Answer questions about prepayment and charges from the lender's terms and the KFS, not from memory.

**What not to promise.**
- No eligibility ("you are eligible", "pre-approved") unless the lender has issued it.
- No amounts before the lender decides.
- No link between credit and the UPI fee or the ₹1 lakh threshold. "Cross ₹1 lakh and you'll get a ₹2 lakh loan" is misleading and is blocked (Compliance rules §3.2).

---

## 5. Insurance

**Who it suits.** Merchants with stock or premises at risk: kiranas, electronics, pharmacies.

**Readiness signals.** Stated concern (theft, fire, flood), recent stock build-up, an existing loan where cover would protect the merchant.

**What not to promise.** Never bundle insurance with a loan or a waiver as a condition. Explain what is covered and what is not, and point to the insurer's policy document.

---

## 6. Plan changes

**Who it suits.** Merchants whose current plan no longer fits: Soundbox rental on a low-volume shop, a settlement cycle that strains cash flow.

**Readiness signals.** Complaints about rental or charges, low volume against the rental cost, cash-flow comments in visits.

**Use in retention.** A plan change can be a cheaper fix than a waiver. Check it before Retention playbook §7.

**What not to promise.** Only plans that exist in the catalogue. No custom terms.
